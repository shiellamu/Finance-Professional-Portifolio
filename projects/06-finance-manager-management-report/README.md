# Finance Manager Management Report

## Purpose

This case study demonstrates how a Finance Manager can consolidate financial performance, forecasting, cash, donor compliance, partner oversight and internal controls into a leadership-level management report.

The emphasis is on decisions and actions rather than simply presenting accounting results.

## Monthly report structure

### 1. Executive financial position

Summarise:

- current expenditure;
- budget utilisation;
- material variances;
- forecast position;
- key financial risks.

### 2. Budget and forecast

Explain:

**Observation → Interpretation → Financial implication → Action**

For example:

> Expenditure is below budget in several activity categories. Finance should establish whether the position reflects implementation timing, changed activity plans or genuine cost reductions before revising the forecast.

### 3. Cash and funding

Cover:

- projected closing cash;
- expected receipts;
- planned payments;
- liquidity pressure;
- funding actions.

### 4. Grant and donor compliance

Summarise:

- reporting status;
- reconciliation status;
- exceptions;
- corrective actions;
- upcoming donor deadlines.

### 5. Partner performance

Include:

- partner reporting timeliness;
- budget utilisation;
- outstanding advances;
- financial exceptions;
- agreed corrective actions.

### 6. Controls and close

Report:

- unreconciled balances;
- ageing items;
- close status;
- system/process issues;
- control improvements.

### 7. Decisions required

End with a short list of decisions or actions requiring leadership attention.

## Finance Manager decision language

Use factual, action-oriented language:

- **Observation:** what the numbers show.
- **Interpretation:** what the result may mean, based on available evidence.
- **Risk:** what could happen if the issue is not addressed.
- **Action:** what should be reviewed, decided or completed.
- **Owner/timing:** who is responsible and by when.

## Example executive summary

The current portfolio position shows expenditure below the approved budget, with the largest variances concentrated in activity-related costs. Finance should validate implementation timing and commitments before concluding that the remaining budget represents genuine savings. Cash forecasts should also be monitored where payment timing and expected receipts create periods of lower liquidity. Material grant, partner and reconciliation exceptions should remain visible until resolved.

## Leadership value

A Finance Manager adds value by connecting accounting information with programme delivery, funding, compliance, risk and management decisions. The report should therefore be concise enough for leadership while retaining a clear evidence trail for follow-up.

All scenarios and figures are synthetic.
