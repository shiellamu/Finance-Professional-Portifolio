# Cash Management Actions

## Forecast interpretation

The six-month forecast shows a progressive reduction in projected cash through May, when closing cash reaches 45,000. June receipts improve the projected position to 85,000.

The forecast should be treated as an early-warning tool rather than a static report.

## Monthly review

| Month | Closing cash | Management focus |
|---|---:|---|
| Jan | 155,000 | Confirm baseline assumptions |
| Feb | 115,000 | Monitor receipts and planned payments |
| Mar | 100,000 | Review payment timing and commitments |
| Apr | 115,000 | Validate forecast against implementation |
| May | 45,000 | Review liquidity risk and funding timing |
| Jun | 85,000 | Confirm receipt timing and rebuild buffer |

## Questions for management

- Are May receipts sufficiently reliable?
- Which May payments are contractual or time-critical?
- Can discretionary payments be rescheduled?
- Are there unrecorded commitments?
- Is additional funding required under the organisation's cash policy?
- What minimum operating cash level should be maintained?

## Finance Manager perspective

The purpose of cash forecasting is to create time for management action. A forecast that identifies pressure only after payments become due is less useful than one that highlights the issue early and identifies the assumptions driving it.
