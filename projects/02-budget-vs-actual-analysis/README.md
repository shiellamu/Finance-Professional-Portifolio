# Budget vs Actual Management Report

## Purpose

This case study demonstrates how finance reporting can move beyond reporting variances to provide management with a concise interpretation of financial performance and the actions required.

The analysis uses the synthetic dataset from Project 1.

## Management questions

- Is the current expenditure position consistent with implementation progress?
- Which variances require explanation?
- Which underspends may be timing-related?
- What should management consider in the next forecast?
- Which issues require follow-up with programme or partner teams?

## Management reporting approach

1. Establish the overall budget and actual position.
2. Identify material category variances.
3. Separate timing, implementation and potential structural variances.
4. Review commitments and expected future expenditure.
5. Translate findings into forecast and management actions.
6. Assign follow-up ownership and timing.

## Key management messages

- Annual expenditure is below budget, with utilisation of 81.5%.
- Travel, training and monitoring and evaluation require implementation-timing review.
- Subcontract utilisation is high and should be monitored against commitments and remaining invoices.
- The overall underspend should not be treated as available savings until planned activities, commitments and implementation changes are confirmed.
- The forecast should be updated using realistic activity timing and known commitments.

## Finance Manager value

The report demonstrates the ability to turn financial data into decision support: identify the issue, interpret its likely cause, state the financial implication, and define the next management action.
