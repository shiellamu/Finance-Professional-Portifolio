# Variance Analysis

## Annual position

- Total annual budget: **1,100,000**
- Total actual expenditure: **896,000**
- Total underspend: **204,000**
- Overall utilisation: **81.5%**

The portfolio-level position indicates material underspending, but the financial result alone does not explain whether funds are genuinely available or whether implementation is behind plan.

## Category analysis

### Personnel
Budget: 420,000; Actual: 382,000; Variance: 38,000 underspend; Utilisation: 91.0%.

Personnel spending is relatively close to budget. The remaining variance should be reconciled to staffing plans, vacancies, start dates and approved changes before revising the forecast.

### Travel and field operations
Budget: 180,000; Actual: 126,000; Variance: 54,000 underspend; Utilisation: 70.0%.

This is the largest absolute variance. The first management question should be whether field activities were delayed, reduced, rescheduled or delivered through a different approach.

### Training and workshops
Budget: 140,000; Actual: 91,000; Variance: 49,000 underspend; Utilisation: 65.0%.

The low utilisation warrants review of the training calendar, procurement status, participant plans and implementation progress. A low spend level may represent delayed activity rather than a permanent reduction in need.

### Subcontracts
Budget: 210,000; Actual: 195,000; Variance: 15,000 underspend; Utilisation: 92.9%.

This is the highest utilisation category. Finance should monitor outstanding commitments, contracts and expected invoices to avoid a late-period funding pressure.

### Monitoring and evaluation
Budget: 150,000; Actual: 102,000; Variance: 48,000 underspend; Utilisation: 68.0%.

The variance should be assessed against the M&E implementation calendar, planned evaluations and procurement milestones.

## Management conclusion

The most important financial issue is not simply that total expenditure is below budget. The key question is whether the remaining budget is aligned with the activities still expected to be delivered.

The next forecast should incorporate:
- confirmed implementation timing;
- open commitments;
- procurement status;
- expected invoices;
- approved programme changes; and
- risks that could affect remaining-period spend.

This converts variance analysis from a reporting exercise into a management decision-support process.
