# Financial Performance Analysis

## Executive purpose

This case study demonstrates how a finance professional can move from budget-versus-actual data to management insight, risk identification and practical financial actions.

The analysis uses a fully synthetic programme-finance dataset. It demonstrates skills relevant to donor-funded and international development environments without exposing real organisational, donor, partner or employee information.

## Business question

Management wants to understand:
- Where spending is materially below budget.
- Which cost categories require investigation rather than being treated as savings.
- Whether implementation timing may explain variances.
- Where management attention or forecast revisions are required.

## Analysis approach

1. Compare annual budget with actual expenditure.
2. Calculate absolute and percentage variance.
3. Review utilisation by cost category.
4. Identify the largest variance drivers.
5. Distinguish timing/implementation issues from potential genuine savings.
6. Translate findings into management actions.

## Key results

| Cost category | Budget | Actual | Underspend | Utilisation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Personnel | 420,000 | 382,000 | 38,000 | 91.0% |
| Travel & field operations | 180,000 | 126,000 | 54,000 | 70.0% |
| Training & workshops | 140,000 | 91,000 | 49,000 | 65.0% |
| Subcontracts | 210,000 | 195,000 | 15,000 | 92.9% |
| Monitoring & evaluation | 150,000 | 102,000 | 48,000 | 68.0% |
| **Total** | **1,100,000** | **896,000** | **204,000** | **81.5%** |

### Management interpretation

- Total expenditure is 81.5% of annual budget, leaving 204,000 unspent.
- Travel and field operations has the largest absolute underspend at 54,000.
- Training has the lowest utilisation at 65.0%, making implementation timing and planned activity delivery important areas for review.
- Monitoring and evaluation is 48,000 below budget and should be assessed against the implementation calendar and expected deliverables.
- Subcontracts have the highest utilisation at 92.9%, so remaining funding and commitments should be monitored closely.
- The overall underspend should not automatically be treated as a saving. Management should first establish whether activities are delayed, cancelled, rescheduled or genuinely costing less than forecast.

## Management actions

1. Confirm whether travel, training and M&E variances are caused by delayed activities or changed implementation plans.
2. Review commitments and procurement status before reducing forecasts.
3. Update the remaining-period forecast using realistic activity timing.
4. Review subcontractor commitments and expected invoices because utilisation is already high.
5. Escalate material implementation or funding risks to programme leadership.
6. Document the explanation and action owner for significant variances.
