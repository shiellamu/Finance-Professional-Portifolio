# Budget & Variance Analysis

**Portfolio project · Practice case · Fictional data**

## Business question

Where are actual results materially different from budget, what patterns appear across the year, and what information should management investigate before taking action?

## Dataset

Source: `datasets/budget_vs_actual.csv`

The dataset contains monthly budget and actual revenue and expense figures for 2026.

> **Public-safe note:** all figures are fictional and are provided for portfolio practice.

## Analysis scope

Build the analysis in Excel:

- Revenue variance and variance %
- Expense variance and variance %
- Monthly contribution
- Annual budget vs actual totals
- Largest revenue deviations
- Largest expense deviations
- Materiality and recurring patterns
- Management commentary

### Core formulas

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Revenue variance | Actual Revenue − Budget Revenue |
| Revenue variance % | Revenue Variance ÷ Budget Revenue |
| Expense variance | Actual Expenses − Budget Expenses |
| Expense variance % | Expense Variance ÷ Budget Expenses |
| Contribution | Actual Revenue − Actual Expenses |

Document whether positive or negative expense variance is favourable under your chosen business logic.

## Required portfolio evidence

1. Completed Excel variance model
2. Annual summary
3. Monthly variance table
4. At least three material revenue variances
5. At least three material expense variances
6. Dashboard
7. One-page management commentary

## Dashboard specification

Recommended visuals:

- Budget vs actual revenue
- Budget vs actual expenses
- Monthly contribution
- Revenue variance %
- Expense variance %
- Material variance table

Use clear labels and conditional formatting. Avoid decorative visuals that do not support a finance decision.

## Commentary framework

**What happened → Materiality → Possible explanation/hypothesis → Required follow-up**

Separate evidence from assumptions.

## Limitations

The dataset does not include volume, pricing, headcount, procurement, FX, programme activity or operational drivers. Explanations therefore require further evidence.
